Client-focused regression case study

Ames Housing Statistical Consulting

A consulting analysis translating a multiple regression model into practical pricing rules for real-estate agents.

The problem

How much should a comparable home’s expected price change for an additional 100 square feet of living area, and what is the adjusted premium for the NridgHt neighborhood relative to OldTown?

Approach

The analysis used multiple linear regression with sale price as the response and living area, overall quality, and neighborhood as predictors. Diagnostics identified heteroscedasticity, so HC3 robust standard errors were used as a sensitivity analysis.

Results

Holding quality and neighborhood constant, an additional 100 square feet corresponded to an estimated increase of about $5,600. A comparable home in NridgHt was estimated to sell for roughly $104,000 more than one in OldTown.

Limitations and next steps

The model is intended as a practical rule of thumb rather than a complete appraisal system. It does not include every property characteristic, market timing, or nonlinear relationships, and residual variability remains meaningful.

Project snapshot

  • 1,460 home sales
  • 25 neighborhoods
  • Model R²: 0.787
  • HC3 robust standard errors
