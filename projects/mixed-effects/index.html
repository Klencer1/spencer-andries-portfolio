Linear mixed-effects modeling

Vitamin E, Copper, and Pig Growth

An analysis of longitudinal pig weights accounting for repeated observations and nesting of pigs within litters.

The problem

Do vitamin E, copper, time, or their interactions affect pig weight, while accounting for repeated observations and shared litter-level variability?

Approach

A linear mixed-effects model included vitamin E, copper, time, and interactions as fixed effects, with random intercepts for litter and pig nested within litter. Estimated marginal means, Tukey-adjusted comparisons, residual diagnostics, Brown-Forsythe testing, and a Box-Cox transformation were explored.

Results

Weight increased strongly over time, while vitamin E, copper, and their interactions were not statistically significant. Treatment means and confidence intervals were similar, and most random-effect variation occurred between pigs within litters.

Limitations and next steps

The course model did not fully specify a repeated-measures covariance structure for time. Residual tails remained non-normal despite transformation, so the analysis appropriately notes the model’s structural and diagnostic limitations.

Project snapshot

  • 72 pigs from 24 litters
  • 12 weekly measurements
  • Nested random effects
  • 65.7% variance at pig-within-litter level
