Quantifying and Predicting Flight Delay Propagation

A reproducible Python analysis linking sequential flights from the same aircraft to measure and predict downstream delays.

The problem

To what extent do delays propagate between sequential flights of the same aircraft, and which operational factors best predict whether the next flight will be delayed?

Approach

Flights were ordered by aircraft tail number, time fields were converted from HHMM to datetimes, overnight travel was handled, invalid aircraft sequences were removed, and propagation variables were engineered. Logistic regression, linear regression, and L1-regularized models were evaluated using train-test splits.

Results

A flight following a delayed inbound aircraft had roughly a 70% chance of delay versus about 10% when the inbound flight was on time. Previous arrival delay was the dominant predictor. Adding congestion, time-of-day, and airline improved AUC from 0.777 to 0.828.

Limitations and next steps

The classification model was highly specific but had limited sensitivity because delays were uncommon. The regression explained about one-third of variation, suggesting that granular weather, crew, mechanical, and network features could substantially improve prediction.

Project snapshot

  • 5.8 million raw U.S. flights
  • ~500,000 valid linked flight records
  • Full logistic model AUC: 0.828
  • Regression R²: 0.319
